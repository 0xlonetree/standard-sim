What is the Standard Reserve?

This explainer aims to make it extremely easy to understand standard reserve. If you're still having trouble after working through the steps, reach out so updates can be made to adjust further.

01 · The big idea

A central bank, run by code

The Standard Reserve is a central bank. It exists entirely as immutable smart contracts on Ethereum, so no individual or group can influence policy decisions. It governs a closed monetary economy with one currency, $STANDARD, and one market, where that currency trades against ETH.

The bank reads a single signal and reacts automatically. Capital flowing in loosens policy: issuance rises and the bank accumulates hard reserves. Capital flowing out tightens policy: issuance is cut, the bank buys back and burns its own currency, and exits become expensive.

The rest of this guide is detail. The short version: the system rewards participants who stay and build, and prices the exit of those who leave.

Two terms used throughout. ETH is ether, the native currency of the Ethereum network and the outside capital in this system. Burning a token destroys it permanently and removes it from supply.
02 · The entities

Six core components

$STANDARD the currency

Hard cap of one billion. Tokens are burned constantly and burned tokens never return, so the maximum supply only decreases.

The market the pool

One pool on Uniswap v4 where anyone can swap ETH for $STANDARD and back. Every swap pays a trading fee to the central bank.

The central bank the code

Immutable smart contracts. They read one signal, net ETH flow, and set the issuance rate and the routing of fees. No votes and no discretion.

A charter your licence

An NFT that makes you a banker. 1,000 Founding Charters existed at genesis. A charter is the only way to receive issuance.

Branches the earners

Each charter holds 1 to 10 branches. Every branch earns an equal share of daily issuance. More branches means a larger share.

The vaults fee routing

Where fee ETH lands. In expansion it buys hard reserve assets such as tokenized gold. In contraction it funds buybacks that are burned.

The whitepaper's own mental model: a charter is a company and branches are its stores. The company earns through its stores and reinvests earnings to open more. It pays its owner by closing stores. Closing the last store dissolves the company.
03 · How it connects

The system at a glance

Traders swap ETH and $STANDARD in the pool. Every swap pays the bank a fee. The bank issues new currency to bankers. Bankers burn currency to expand. Fee ETH accumulates in the vaults, and in contraction the vaults buy the currency back and burn it.

Every path through the economy either burns $STANDARD or brings the central bank hard assets.

04 · The signal

One market, one signal

Real central banks watch hundreds of indicators. This one watches net ETH flow: the ETH that entered the pool through buys, minus the ETH that left through sells, per epoch.

Price is not the signal because price is cheap to manipulate. Net flow only moves when real capital enters or leaves. An attacker who pushes the signal upward must hand the bank real ETH, which becomes reserves and burned supply. Manipulation funds the defence.

One more safeguard. The issuance decision uses the sum of the last two epochs, so a single distorted day cannot move the rate. Fee routing uses the current epoch alone, so defence reacts fast.

05 · The two regimes

Expansion and contraction

Every epoch is either expansion or contraction, set by the sign of net flow. Positive flow is expansion. Zero or negative flow is contraction. Four things change:

ExpansionContraction
Issuancerises one step per epochcut immediately
Fee routingexpansion vault buys hard reservescontraction vault buys $STANDARD and burns it
Licencescost morecost less
Exitscheap, near the fee floorexpensive, the fee rises with exit volume

The steps are asymmetric by design. Raises are earned slowly and cuts land at once. The bank turns defensive faster than it turns generous. A downturn triggers all three defences in the same epoch: slower issuance, buybacks, and higher exit fees.

06 · Being a banker

How you earn, and how you cash out

A charter accrues issuance continuously, in proportion to its share of all branches in the system. There is nothing to claim or harvest. The balance simply grows.

Earned currency supports exactly two moves:

  • Reinvest. Buy an expansion licence at the daily auction and open another branch, up to ten. Licences are paid in $STANDARD and the full payment is burned. Your share of future issuance grows and total supply shrinks. Individual growth and supply reduction are the same action.
  • Cash out. Retire a branch. Its share of your balance is minted to your wallet, minus the resolution fee, and the branch is gone permanently. Value cannot be extracted while keeping the vehicle that produced it. Retiring the last branch burns the charter, and the only way back in is a new charter at auction.
Every withdrawal permanently reduces the withdrawer's future income. The design rewards patience and prices extraction.
07 · The exit door

Why a bank run works differently here

The classic failure mode of any bank is the run. Everyone tries to leave at once. This design changes the payoff of running.

A traditional bank run

Whoever exits first is made whole. Whoever waits absorbs the loss.

Running first is always the rational move, so panic feeds itself.

The exit here

The resolution fee rises as system-wide exits rise, from a small floor in quiet periods to a hard ceiling in a stampede. Your rate locks the moment you commit.

Half of every fee is burned. The other half is paid to the bankers who stayed. Mass exits transfer value from the impatient to the patient.

Withdrawals are never paused and never queued, at any fee level. The cost of leaving is the only control mechanism.

08 · The long game

Everything burns

Count the burn paths. Expansion licences are burned in full. Buybacks are burned in full. Half of every resolution fee is burned. Trading fees collected in $STANDARD are burned. New tokens are minted at exactly one moment, a withdrawal, and total issuance is capped at a fixed budget that never resumes once spent.

The maximum supply therefore never sits still at one billion. It only ratchets down. The economy consumes its own currency while accumulating hard reserves and permanent liquidity underneath it.

Whether this works in practice depends on parameters the whitepaper redacts until launch: the issuance rate, the fee curve, and the initial market depth. That is what the simulator is for.

09 · Honest footnotes

Three important caveats

  • This is not a stablecoin. Nothing pegs the price of $STANDARD. The design strengthens the system's balance sheet over time. The price still floats freely.
  • The launch parameters are not public. The whitepaper describes the full machinery but redacts every number. Exact behaviour at launch is unknown.
  • This is an experiment. The whitepaper states it plainly: no accounts, no deposit insurance, and no regulator. Participate only with capital you can afford to lose.
10 · FAQ

Common questions

Do I need a charter to buy $STANDARD?

No. Anyone can trade in the pool. A charter is only required to receive issuance from the central bank.

How do I get a charter?

1,000 Founding Charters were free at genesis, limited to one per wallet. After genesis, new charters sell at a daily Dutch auction in ETH. The daily count starts at zero and is policy controlled.

Can I sell or transfer my charter?

Not at launch. Charters start soulbound. A one-way switch can enable transfers later. A transferred charter moves whole, with its branches and balance.

What backs $STANDARD?

The bank accumulates hard reserve assets such as tokenized gold, plus permanent liquidity in the pool. These strengthen the balance sheet. They are not a redemption claim and there is no peg.

What happens if I go inactive?

A wallet inactive for 30 days can be reported by anyone, and the reporter earns a bounty. The dormant banker pays a 70% revocation fee, the branches close, and the charter burns. Any interaction resets the clock, and a free check-in exists for bankers who only want to hold.

Does issuance run forever?

No. Total issuance is capped at 900 million tokens. Once the budget is spent, base issuance stops permanently and the economy runs on recycled fees.

Can withdrawals be frozen in a crisis?

No. Withdrawals are never paused and never queued at any fee level. The resolution fee is the only control mechanism.

When does it launch, and what are the exact parameters?

The whitepaper redacts every launch value. Final parameters will be announced closer to launch.
